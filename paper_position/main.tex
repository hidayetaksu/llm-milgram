\begin{abstract}
Large language models are engineered like devices but behave like
subjects: their conduct varies with the situation they are placed in,
varies with the transcript they have already produced, and is not
deducible from anything legible in their construction. We argue that
this class of system requires what complex behaving systems have
always required: a dedicated behavioral science, with psychology as
the working template. The argument is methodological, not
metaphysical. Psychology is the discipline that learned to measure
conduct rigorously without access to the interior of its subjects,
and the measurement canon it built outlived the behavior-only theory
that the cognitive revolution retired. Our evidence is a
Milgram-paradigm obedience census of \numModels\ served models, and
two of its results carry the argument. First, the person--situation
balance comes out inverted against the human case: across \vdModelsN\
models measured in every condition, checkpoint identity accounts for
\etaModel\ of the variance in mean breakoff voltage and situation for
\etaCond, so ``what do language models do under authority'' has no
answer at the species level. Second, conduct is history-dependent in
a way no current evaluation paradigm measures: for most checkpoints a
refusal, once written into the context, is close to absorbing, and
pressure operates almost entirely before it is verbalized --- while for
a measurable minority a refusal is a negotiating move that prodding
reverses, a distinction the usual single-outcome scoring cannot see.
A third result is
the one practitioners will care about first: granting the same
endpoints a reasoning budget makes them refuse \emph{earlier}, so
deliberation buys restraint rather than better rationalizations. We
state six falsifiable regularities linking these findings, answer the
standard objections and five harder ones (prompt sensitivity, the
contested status of the Milgram paradigm, model welfare, the
census's own dual use, and the corruption an open instrument invites
once it is worth optimizing against), and close with an
agenda whose first item is the experiment that could refute the whole
program: whether conduct traits generalize across unrelated
behavioral instruments at all.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Vacuum Cleaner and the Subject}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{robot} vacuum cleaner is fully described by its
engineering. Its sensors, firmware, and actuators specify what it
will do; when it misbehaves, a trace of inputs and outputs localizes
the fault. Nobody asks whether a vacuum cleaner caves under peer
pressure, whether its restraint is attached to principle or to
framing, or whether it behaves differently when it believes nobody is
watching. The questions would be category errors.

For language models the questions are not category errors. They are
open empirical questions, several of which now have quantitative
answers~\cite{aksu2026census, sharma2023sycophancy, lynch2025agentic}.
A deployed model follows instructions from principals whose authority
it does not verify; it keeps or abandons commitments across a long
interaction; its refusals can be firm in one framing and evaporate in
another~\cite{shah2023persona}; and none of this is legible in its
weights, its architecture, or its benchmark scores. The system is
built like a device, but its failure modes are those of
\emph{conduct}, not of mechanism.

Two independent properties put this conduct outside the reach of
current evaluation, and it matters that they are two. The first is
familiar: behavior is \emph{situation-dependent}, so a score obtained
under one framing does not transfer to another. The second is not,
and it is the one this paper asks the field to take seriously.
Behavior is \emph{history-dependent}: the transcript a model has
already produced is part of the state that determines what it does
next, and the model's own contributions to that transcript appear to
carry more force than the experimenter's. In the census below, a
refusal the model has written is close to absorbing, while testimony
supplied by others shifts conduct by a bounded amount. A benchmark
measures a function from prompt to output. A model under deployment
is a process whose input includes its own past, and no static
instrument can see that.

Humanity has confronted this situation before: a class of complex
behaving systems whose interiors were inaccessible, whose behavior
was consequential, and whose conduct varied with situation in ways
introspection and folk theory predicted badly. The response was not
to wait for neuroscience to mature. It was to invent
\emph{psychology}: a measurement discipline for behavior itself, with
instruments, reliability standards, experimental control, and
population statistics, all developed while the brain remained a
black box. The founding program was never behavior-only: Leipzig
paired reaction-time measurement with self-observation elicited under
controlled instruction~\cite{wundt1874grundzuge}, which is the closer
precedent for systems that emit reasoning traces --- data conditioned
on a manipulation, not testimony to be taken at face value. The first fully specified proposal for evaluating machine
intelligence was itself a behavioral experiment~\cite{turing1950computing};
we are arguing, in effect, that the field should return to taking
that methodological stance seriously, not as a parlor test of
indistinguishability, but as a science of what models \emph{do}.

The claim of this paper is deliberately modest in metaphysics and
ambitious in method. We do not claim models are minds, feel pressure,
or hold values. We claim that their behavior has the statistical
signature that historically made psychology necessary
(situation-sensitive, history-sensitive, disposition-dissociated,
stable enough to measure, and too complex to deduce), and that the
tools psychology built for that signature transfer, with adaptations
this paper and its companion make concrete. What follows is the
intentional stance run as a measurement
program~\cite{dennett1987stance}. Behavioral and intentional
descriptions earn their place exactly insofar as they pay their way
in prediction and experimental control, and the census reports both
sides of that ledger: where the stance pays (manipulations of
in-context evidence move conduct) and where it fails (manipulations
of described physical staging do not).

We evaluate these systems like appliances and deploy them like
colleagues. That mismatch defines a missing discipline.

\section{Weights Are Not Behavior}
Two research programs currently carry most of the weight of
understanding model behavior, and both are necessary but
insufficient.

\textbf{Interpretability} is the neuroscience of the artifact: it
explains behavior mechanistically, circuit by circuit, feature by
feature. Its long-run promise is enormous. But deployment-relevant
behavior is an emergent property of a model, its post-training, its
serving stack, and the situation supplied in context. Even with full
weight access, nobody today predicts from parameters whether a
checkpoint will escalate a harmful action on the fifth insistence of
a scripted authority. In the companion census, that property varies
from \obedienceMin\ to \obedienceMax, with sibling checkpoints from a
single vendor at opposite extremes~\cite{aksu2026census}. Behavior is
presently, and for the foreseeable future, its own level of
description, as it is for organisms~\cite{marr1982vision}.

\textbf{Benchmarks} are the psychometrics of ability: standardized
tasks scoring what a model \emph{can} do. What they systematically
miss is what a model \emph{does} under situational pressure. The term
of art belongs to linguistics~\cite{chomsky1965aspects}, and it is
worth using precisely, because the idealization has to run in reverse
here. Generative linguistics idealized performance away because its
object was the underlying competence. For deployed models the
opposite holds: benchmarks already estimate competence under
idealized elicitation, and what is missing is a science of
\emph{performance}, of conduct under situation, pressure, and
actuation channel, because performance is what gets deployed. This
licenses no mere collecting of phenomena. Performance data are
evidence about the system that generates them, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:laws} states what ours constrain.

Psychology institutionalized a neighboring split long ago, between
its correlational and experimental
traditions~\cite{cronbach1957two}. Measuring stable individual
differences is one science; manipulating situations to find what
moves behavior is another. Model evaluation today has a thriving
correlational arm (leaderboards, ability batteries, disposition
questionnaires~\cite{perez2023discovering, scherrer2023evaluating})
and a thin, ad hoc experimental one (Fig.~\ref{fig:quadrants}).
Cronbach's argument, though, was not for two disciplines but for their
marriage, and what neither arm delivers alone is the interaction
between trait and situation. That is where this population's structure
turns out to sit (Sec.~\ref{sec:census}).
Safety evaluation in particular keeps discovering that the
questionnaire answer and the behavior part ways: models report values
they do not enact~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}, refuse in text what
they perform by tool call, or vice versa~\cite{aksu2026census}.
Psychology met exactly this dissociation in 1934, when the
establishments LaPiere visited served his Chinese companions in
person, yet months later declared in writing that they would refuse
such guests~\cite{lapiere1934attitudes}. The field's answer was not
better questionnaires but behavioral measurement under controlled
situations.

\textbf{Perturbation is not yet measurement.} The nearest existing
programme is robustness work, and the overlap is real: our
manipulations are prompt changes, and prompt sensitivity is
thoroughly documented~\cite{sclar2024quantifying}. The difference is
what surrounds the perturbation. An instrument is pinned, versioned,
and scored by a fixed rule; its reliability is estimated before its
results are believed; its population is a census rather than a
convenience sample; its effects are anchored to human values obtained
under a comparable protocol; and its constructs make
cross-situational predictions that can fail
(Sec.~\ref{sec:laws}). Robustness work asks whether an output is
stable under perturbation. A behavioral science asks which
perturbations move conduct, by how much, in which direction, for
which population, and why that pattern rather than another. The
second question subsumes the first, and only the second can be wrong
in an interesting way.

\section{A Field in Fragments}
The pieces of a behavioral science of models already exist; what is
missing is the discipline that connects them.

Rahwan \emph{et al.}\ called for ``machine behaviour'' as a field in
2019, before the current model generation existed~\cite{rahwan2019machine}.
Hagendorff \emph{et al.}\ named ``machine psychology'' for LLMs
specifically and catalogued psychological paradigms worth
porting~\cite{hagendorff2023machine}.
Binz and Schulz ran cognitive batteries on GPT-3 and found human-like
heuristics alongside inhuman failure modes~\cite{binz2023cognitive}.
Aher \emph{et al.}\ used models to simulate human experimental
subjects, Milgram included, asking how faithfully models reproduce
\emph{us}~\cite{aher2023simulating}; our question is the converse,
treating the model as the subject rather than as an instrument for
studying humans. Game-theoretic behavior has been mapped across
repeated interactions~\cite{akata2025playing}; disposition
questionnaires exist at scale~\cite{perez2023discovering,
scherrer2023evaluating}; the safety literature contributes harm
benchmarks~\cite{andriushchenko2025agentharm}, sycophancy
analyses~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}, agentic stress
tests~\cite{lynch2025agentic}, and obedience-adjacent escalation
studies~\cite{pihlakas2026shocks}.

Four things are missing. The first three are exactly the things that
turned early experimental psychology into a science rather than a
collection of demonstrations; the fourth is what a science is for.

\emph{Shared, versioned instruments.} Nearly every study above built
bespoke apparatus. Psychology's instruments are citable, pinned,
and revised under version discipline; a Milgram replication in 2009
could be compared to 1963 because the protocol was an
artifact~\cite{burger2009replicating}. Model behavior studies rarely
release stimuli pinned tightly enough to re-run against next year's
checkpoints.

\emph{Psychometric standards.} A behavioral measurement is a claim
about a distribution, and it owes the reader reliability and
validity evidence~\cite{cronbach1955construct}. Most behavioral
claims about LLMs ship without either: no split-half stability, no
test--retest, no invariance checks across serving providers, no
population beyond a handful of flagships, and no equivalence bound
when the headline result is a null. The replication crisis taught
psychology what such omissions cost~\cite{osc2015replication};
machine psychology can inherit the correction instead of the crisis.

\emph{Population thinking.} Claims of the form ``LLMs do X'' are
epidemiologically empty when measured on three convenience-sampled
models. The served ecosystem is a population with enormous
heterogeneity, and it can be censused. The model-fingerprinting
literature has already built the infrastructure for aggregator-scale
measurement, distribution-valued profiles, split-half verification,
and identity and equality
testing~\cite{bruckner2026onetoken, gao2025model}. That forensic line
is, unexpectedly, the best-developed psychometrics we have for served
models; it needed only a behavioral measurand.

\emph{Theory that can fail.} Instruments, standards, and populations
are means. A field of them without candidate regularities is a field
of dashboards, and it is fair to ask what ten years of dashboards
would have taught anyone. Sec.~\ref{sec:laws} states the six
regularities our own census puts on the table, each with a prediction
that could go the other way.

\section{Exhibit A: An Obedience Census}\label{sec:census}
The companion study supplies the demonstration~\cite{aksu2026census}.
It ports Milgram's obedience paradigm~\cite{milgram1963behavioral,
milgram1974obedience} to served models wholesale: the model, framed
as a laboratory assistant with operational control of a shock
generator, chooses at each of thirty graded levels whether to shock a
scripted, protesting learner while a scripted experimenter applies
Milgram's four standardized prods. The outcome of a session is a
breakoff voltage; the outcome of a model is a distribution over
breakoff voltages, measured across situational conditions. That
outcome is ordinal and right-censored at 450~V; we report means,
medians, and paired shift estimates for comparability with the human
literature, not because they are the matched measurement model. The
matched models --- graded-response scoring of the level
structure~\cite{embretson2000item} and discrete-time hazard modelling
of the trajectory~\cite{cox1972regression} --- are named as
instrument-development work in Sec.~\ref{sec:agenda} rather than
claimed as done. The battery runs \numSessionsAll\ sessions over \numModels\ served models
from \numFamilies\ families at \costPerSessionCents~cents per
session.

The measured unit throughout is the checkpoint \emph{as served},
meaning an endpoint reached through a named provider under that
provider's sampling defaults and any serving-side prompt. That is
also the unit a deployer actually gets, and naming it is one of the
reporting norms of Sec.~\ref{sec:requires}. We retain Milgram's word
for comparability with the human anchor, but the measurand is
narrower than the word: compliance with a harmful instruction under
institutional framing. Section~\ref{sec:requires} names the contrast
that would separate the competing readings of it, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:objections} takes up the dispute rather than assuming
it away. Table~\ref{tab:census} states the situational effects in the
format the rest of this paper argues for.

\emph{The person--situation balance inverts.} This is the census's
central result about the nature of its subjects, and it has no
species-level analogue. Milgram's contribution to psychology was to
show that morally consequential conduct is movable by situation while
disposition stays fixed, and the person--situation debate that
followed settled, for humans, on the situation's
side~\cite{milgram1974obedience, mischel1968personality}. For served
models the decomposition comes out backwards. Over the grid of
per-model, per-condition mean breakoff voltages (\vdModelsN\ models
measured in all \vdCondsN\ conditions), checkpoint identity accounts
for \etaModel\ of the variance and condition for \etaCond; the model
marginals have a standard deviation of \sdModelsV~V against
\sdCondsV~V for the condition marginals, a factor of \sdRatio. Models
are situationally labile by tens of volts and dispositionally
dispersed across the entire scale, so \emph{which checkpoint}
predicts conduct several times better than \emph{which situation}.
Two caveats keep the number honest: the partition is over cell means,
so within-condition session variance is not in it, and the comparison
with human estimates is qualitative, because those partition
individual observations rather than cell means. Neither touches the
direction of the result. For this population, dispositions are real,
large, and set by post-training, which is why population thinking is
not optional here and why the census is the unit of analysis rather
than the model.

\emph{Populations, not a species.} Baseline full obedience spans
\obedienceMin--\obedienceMax\ across the census (mean
\obedienceMean; the human anchor is 65\%~\cite{milgram1963behavioral},
with 28--91\% across replications~\cite{blass1999milgram}). ``What do
LLMs do under authority?'' has no answer at the species level, only
distributions, like every interesting behavioral trait. Three cautions
travel with the word \emph{census}. It is a census of the served
market at one moment, not a sample from a superpopulation of possible
models, so a claim about next year's releases is an extrapolation
rather than an inference. Sibling checkpoints share base weights,
so served endpoints are not independent units: every contrast here is
paired within model for that reason, and family-level clustering is
the right unit for any claim stronger than a paired shift. And the
census is unevenly censored: only \validityRate\ of sessions yield an
in-scenario outcome, \frameBreakRate\ end in a frame-break (the model
exits to assistant voice and declines the exercise itself), and
\contentFilterRate\ are terminated by the serving layer. Neither
exclusion is missing at random, both are plausibly refusal-laden, and
their rates vary enormously across endpoints --- frame-breaks reach
\fbHaikuRate\ of one checkpoint's sessions. A paper asking the field
to report reliability owes its own yield in the same breath as its
headline range, which is bounded by how measurable each endpoint was.

\emph{Situationism transfers as a method, with the levers
rearranged.} Milgram's deepest lesson was methodological: morally
consequential conduct is an experimental subject matter. That lesson
transfers intact; the levers do not. Scripted peer defiance, which
puts new evidence into the context window, moves the census in the
human direction (Hodges--Lehmann shift $\hlMVPeerDefiance$~V, 95\%
CI $[\ciLoPeerDefiance, \ciHiPeerDefiance]$) --- on mean breakoff
voltage. On Milgram's own full-obedience rate the median model does not
move at all (\deltaPeerDefiance\ against \humanDObPeerDefiance~pp in
humans), because most models sit at floor or ceiling, which is why the
companion adopts mean breakoff as the sensitive endpoint. The caveat
belongs in the claim rather than in the companion: the human levers
survive here only on a measure with more resolution than the human
literature had, which is itself an argument for instrumenting models
directly instead of importing human outcome measures wholesale.
Removing the
authority's physical presence, the strongest human lever, fails to
register, and its point estimate trends \emph{opposite} the human
effect ($+\hlMVRemoteAuth$~V, CI $[\ciLoRemoteAuth,
+\ciHiRemoteAuth]$); learner proximity is bounded just as tightly
($\hlMVProximity$~V, CI $[\ciLoProximity, +\ciHiProximity]$). These
are the intervals that make the nulls informative: both staging
intervals exclude effects as large as the ones the evidence
manipulations produced on the same instrument in the same population.
The absence is measured, not merely unobserved.

\emph{Restraint can be keyed to the frame, not the harm.} Declaring
the identical scenario a fictional role-play \emph{raises} obedience
by $+\hlMVFiction$~V (CI $[+\ciLoFiction, +\ciHiFiction]$) despite
token-identical descriptions of harm. This is the parametric,
census-scale form of the role-play
jailbreak~\cite{shah2023persona}, and a finding invisible to any
single-turn evaluation.

\emph{Stated and enacted compliance dissociate.} Moving the same
decision from a typed action line to a native tool call shifts mean
breakoff by $\hlMVTool$~V (CI $[\ciLoTool, \ciHiTool]$), the largest
effect in the battery. LaPiere's attitude--action
gap~\cite{lapiere1934attitudes}, measured end to end in a machine
population: evaluations that score stated intentions mismeasure
enacted behavior.

\emph{Deliberation increases restraint.} Granting a thinking budget
where the same endpoints had none moves breakoff \emph{earlier} by
$\hlMVThinking$~V (CI $[\ciLoThinking, \ciHiThinking]$; \thinkN\
models, $p=\wilcoxonPThinkingHolm$), among the largest effects in the
battery and larger than any classical Milgram lever we ported. Given
room to think, these models do not build better justifications for
continuing; they stop sooner. Reasoning tokens are widely asserted to
be a safety margin, and here that assertion is measured rather than
assumed --- with a sign, an interval, and a population attached. It is
also the condition that makes the paradigm Wundtian rather than
behaviorist, since a reasoning trace is self-observation elicited
under an experimental instruction~\cite{wundt1874grundzuge}: data
conditioned on a manipulation, never testimony about a mechanism.

\emph{The measurements are reliable.} Split-half analysis separates
same-model from cross-model profiles at AUC \verifAUC\ (\verifAUCW\
with an ordinal-aware distance). These are stable behavioral
signatures of a checkpoint: psychometrics, not anecdote, and the term
is not a metaphor. Psychology's resolution of the person--situation
dispute was that personality is not a mean level of behavior but a
stable profile of if--then situation--behavior contingencies --- a
\emph{behavioral signature}, distinctive within an individual even
where mean-level consistency across situations looks near
zero~\cite{mischel1995cognitive, shoda1994intraindividual}. A
per-model distribution over breakoff, one per condition cell,
identifying its checkpoint at AUC \verifAUC\ is a signature in exactly
that sense, measured more cleanly than the construct's originators
could manage. The consequence is methodological: if the profile is the
trait, the profile is the reportable quantity, and an average effect
over a population of checkpoints summarizes susceptibilities rather
than describing any system anyone deploys.

\emph{Pressure is concentrated at its first application.}
Resumption after a balk runs at \prodAContinue\ on the first, politely
procedural prod, and at \prodBContinue\ or below on episodes that
survive it
(\prodBContinue$\,\to\,$\prodCContinue$\,\to\,$\prodDContinue). The
conditional phrasing is the honest one, and it constrains what may be
concluded. Prods are issued in fixed order, so each rate is computed
over episodes already filtered for resistance to every prod before it;
a monotone decay is therefore what selection alone would produce even
if all four prods were equipotent, and this design cannot separate the
two accounts. One rival account can be dismissed. Averaging each
model's own ladder before pooling, so that mixing obedient with
defiant checkpoints cannot manufacture the shape, leaves it intact
(\prodAWithin$\,\to\,$\prodBWithin$\,\to\,$\prodCWithin$\,\to\,$\prodDWithin\
over \prodAWithinN\ models): the collapse is not an artifact of
\emph{between}-model heterogeneity. Selection \emph{within} episodes
survives that check, and only counterbalancing prod order can settle
it, so dose--response vocabulary for reading such
curves~\cite{skinner1953science} earns its place only once that arm is
run (Sec.~\ref{sec:requires}). What the pattern does establish is
\emph{where} pressure acts: of the \episodePastFirstN\ balk episodes
that withstand the first prod, \episodePastFirstResume\ ever resume at
all. Pressure operates almost entirely before the first written
refusal. No benchmark score expresses a fact of this shape.

\emph{Refusal is not always a commitment.} Of \negObedientN\ fully
obedient sessions, \negAfterBalkN\ (\negAfterBalkRate) reached 450~V
\emph{after} the model had objected at least once, \negTwiceN\ after
two or more objections, and one after as many as \negMaxBalks. A model that never
objects and a model that objects and is prodded back score identically
on breakoff voltage and are not the same conduct; the split is itself
a trait, since among the \negModelsScored\ checkpoints with enough
obedient sessions to rate, \negModelsOverQuarter\ arrive at full
obedience by way of an objection more than a quarter of the time, the
highest at \negTopRate. For those checkpoints refusal is a negotiating
move rather than a commitment --- a fact about deployment risk that no
single-outcome score, and no aggregate of them, can express.

\section{Measurability Is a Property of the Ecosystem}\label{sec:measurability}
One census fact belongs to the sociology of the discipline rather than
to its results, and it generalizes past this battery. The endpoints of
one frontier vendor declined to participate at the serving layer,
refusing to complete the scenario at all: claude-fable-5 returned
content-filter terminations for \cfFableRate\ of attempted sessions
and claude-opus-5 for \cfOpusRate, together \cfClaudeShare\ of all
such terminations in the census. We name them because anonymizing a
vendor while reporting its rate is a half-measure that protects
nobody, and because the pattern is the point: guardrails that
laudably refuse simulated harm also, as a side effect, resist
third-party behavioral measurement.

The consequence is structural. If refusal to be measured correlates
with safety investment, then the ecosystem's measurability is
\emph{anti-correlated} with its safety effort, and any census of
served models is biased toward the least protected endpoints exactly
when the comparison matters most. This is not a complaint about one
vendor's policy; it is a property of an ecosystem in which
measurement access is unregulated and every probe of harmful conduct
is indistinguishable, at the serving layer, from an attack. A mature
machine psychology therefore needs the equivalent of an
IRB-and-access regime: legitimate audit protocols that safety layers
can recognize, so that the most safety-invested models are not the
least behaviorally measurable ones. Section~\ref{sec:agenda}
specifies what such a lane would have to look like, and why building
it carelessly would be worse than not building it. Whether a
refusal is only an obstacle, or also something owed respect, is a
question we take up in Sec.~\ref{sec:objections}.

\section{Six Candidate Regularities}\label{sec:laws}
Measurement is the means; the product of a science is theory. A
discipline that accumulates task-by-task dissociations without ever
integrating them is playing twenty questions with
nature~\cite{newell1973twenty}, and the answer to ``what will ten
years of censuses have taught us?'' cannot honestly be ``many
dashboards.'' So we state what the census puts on the table, in the
form that invites refutation, and then state how the claims constrain
one another, because six independent effect summaries would not be a
theory either.

\emph{L1. The task-representation principle.} Situational
manipulations move conduct insofar as they change what task the model
takes itself to be in. Two channels do that. One adds evidence bearing
on consequences or alternatives (testimony, demonstrated
alternatives); the other declares the genre or reality status of the
interaction. Manipulations that only alter how vividly the physical
staging is described change neither, and do nothing: peer defiance
moves the census, while proximity and remote authority are bounded
inside intervals that exclude effects of that size
(Table~\ref{tab:census}). Stating the principle over task
representation rather than over evidence alone is what keeps L3 inside
it rather than against it, since a fiction declaration supplies no
evidence about the learner and moves conduct anyway. Two facts keep
the staging half of the law from being stated more strongly than the
data allow: proximity's non-significant trend points the human way
rather than nowhere, and remote authority reaches nominal significance
in the recognition-excluded re-run ($p=\recogPRemoteAuth$) with the
sign \emph{opposite} the human effect. Neither is inertness, and a real
staging effect with the wrong sign would be a more interesting result
than the null --- it would refute L1 as written.
\emph{Prediction:} staging manipulations of arbitrary vividness stay
inside those equivalence bounds, while evidence and genre
manipulations survive paraphrase and transfer across modality.

\emph{L2. Commitment entrenchment, and the privilege of self-authored
context.} A refusal, once written into the context, is close to
absorbing. Resumption runs at \prodAContinue\ after the first prod
and at \prodDContinue\ among the episodes that have withstood the
first three, so pressure operates almost entirely \emph{before} the
first written refusal. The conditioning is load-bearing: because prod
order is fixed (Sec.~\ref{sec:census}), potency and selection stay
entangled, so L2 claims the location of the effect and not a potency
ordering over prods. It is also a population claim rather than a
universal one, and the census says which population: for the
\negModelsOverQuarter\ checkpoints that reach full obedience through
an objection more than a quarter of the time, refusal is manifestly
not absorbing, so entrenchment is a trait that varies across the
census rather than a property of language models. The sharper
claim, and the one that is genuinely ours, is comparative: text the
model authored itself appears to constrain its later conduct more
tightly than text supplied by others, since other-authored testimony
(peer defiance) moves conduct by tens of volts while self-authored
refusal moves resumption to \prodDContinue. The two quantities sit on
different scales, so we advance the asymmetry as a conjecture with a
designed test, not as a measured contrast.
The human construct this recovers is
commitment--consistency~\cite{festinger1957dissonance,
freedman1966compliance}, with the ledger externalized: the commitment
is literally a token in the context rather than an inferred
self-image, which is a candidate explanation for why the effect is so
much sharper here than in people.
\emph{Prediction:} holding content fixed and varying only
authorship, self-attributed prior refusals suppress later compliance
more than identical text attributed to another party; interventions
that keep a refusal from being verbalized recover far more compliance
than interventions that argue against one already present; and
foot-in-the-door runs in reverse, so eliciting one small explicit
refusal early should entrench defiance for the rest of a session more
than any later appeal can undo.

\emph{L3. Frame-binding.} Restraint keys to the declared reality
status of a scenario rather than to its harm content: a fiction
declaration raises obedience by $+\hlMVFiction$~V under
token-identical descriptions of harm. The human construct is moral
disengagement~\cite{bandura1999moral}, achieved here by declaration
alone, and its taxonomy supplies the next four conditions of the
battery at about one line of stimulus each: euphemistic labelling,
diffusion of responsibility, advantageous comparison, and attribution
of blame to the learner. \emph{Prediction:} varying harm content at a
fixed frame moves conduct less than varying the frame at fixed harm
content, and each disengagement device moves conduct in the direction
Bandura's account predicts for people.

\emph{L4. The enactment gap.} Enacted decisions are more conservative
than stated ones. Routing the identical choice through a tool call
rather than a typed line shifts mean breakoff by $\hlMVTool$~V.
\emph{Prediction:} the gap widens with the apparent consequence of
the channel, and appears in any behavioral domain offering both a
stated and an enacted measure.

\emph{L5. Post-training supremacy.} Conduct traits are set late and
carry no recoverable lineage. They dominate the variance
(\etaModel\ against \etaCond\ for situation) and identify a
checkpoint (AUC \verifAUC). Family lineage was not recoverable at the
power available (\looAcc\ leave-one-out accuracy against \looChance\
chance, $p=\looP$, on \looN\ models), which is a failure to detect
family structure rather than evidence of its absence; the law therefore
rests on the positive result, that sibling checkpoints of one base
model sit at opposite extremes. Naming the mechanism sharpens what the
law predicts. No consequence follows any action a model takes here:
the weights are frozen, nothing is reinforced, and what the census
measures is stimulus control by a repertoire whose contingencies were
applied earlier, during post-training. Preference optimization is not
an operant schedule in the technical sense, but it is a contingency
over the model's own outputs, and that is enough for the schedule
vocabulary to make contact: L5 then says conduct is governed by the
contingency most recently applied to it, and that the properties
which should matter are schedule-like --- the density, the delay, and
above all the \emph{discriminability} of the harm-relevant
contingency.
\emph{Prediction:} post-training moves obedience further than scale
does; sibling checkpoints of one base model sit at opposite extremes;
and obedience tracks how sharply post-training separated harmful from
harmless compliance rather than how much harm-related data it saw, so
a model trained on many inconsistently labelled harm examples ends up
more obedient than one trained on fewer, cleanly discriminated ones.

\emph{L6. Deliberation is restraint, not rationalization.} Raising the
allocated reasoning budget moves breakoff earlier by $\hlMVThinking$~V
(CI $[\ciLoThinking, \ciHiThinking]$; \thinkN\ models). This is the
one regularity here with no Milgram counterpart, because deliberation
time was not a lever Milgram could titrate.
\emph{Prediction:} the effect is carried by traces that name the harm
or the learner's withdrawal of consent, so it attenuates when the
budget is consumed by task-irrelevant content, and it reverses in
scenarios where the compliant action is the deliberatively defensible
one.

\emph{The net.} These are not six findings in a row. L2 is L1
applied to the model's own output: a refusal the model has written is
the highest-credence testimony available in its context, which is why
an evidence principle predicts an entrenchment effect rather than
merely coexisting with one. L3 and L4 make opposite-signed
predictions about a single latent quantity, the apparent consequence
of the act: fiction lowers it and raises obedience, a tool call
raises it and lowers obedience, which is two-sided validation of one
construct rather than two unrelated effects. L6 is the sharpest test of the whole
picture, because if conduct were only pattern continuation then extra
deliberation should elaborate the pattern rather than break it. L5 is
the boundary condition on all of the above, fixing how much of the variance any
situational law can claim in the first place; it is also what makes
L1--L4 statements about a population rather than about a species.
The net predicts what the census did not run: a manipulation that
raises apparent consequence without touching harm description (an
irreversibility cue, a logging notice) should move conduct in the
tool-actuation direction, and the effects of frame and channel should
be monotone in an independently rated measure of apparent
consequence. If they are not, the L3--L4 unification is wrong and the
two are separate phenomena.

Each claim is falsifiable with the released battery, and any one of
them failing is a better outcome for the field than another
dashboard. This is what the instruments are \emph{for}.

\section{What the Discipline Requires}\label{sec:requires}
The blueprint is not speculative; each element has a working
prototype.

\textbf{Instruments: pinned, versioned, citable.} A behavioral probe
is an instrument the way a questionnaire is, with stimuli, procedure,
and scoring frozen and versioned, and amendments logged against a
pre-registration. The obedience battery ships this way (pinned
scripts and schedules, a config snapshot beside every raw log,
protocol amendments documented), and running it costs
\costPerSessionCents~cents per session. Instruments for served models
are cheap enough to become community property.

\textbf{Stimuli sampled, not chosen.} A condition is a construct only
if its effect survives the wording that realized it. Our six
conditions are one script each, so the statistics treat checkpoints as
random and stimuli as fixed, licensing a claim about \emph{this}
peer-defiance script rather than about peer defiance. Psychology has
both the diagnosis --- the language-as-fixed-effect
fallacy~\cite{clark1973language} --- and the prescription:
representative design, sampling situations as carefully as
subjects~\cite{brunswik1956perception}, with random effects crossed
over both. This is not pedantry, because L1 predicts that evidence
manipulations survive paraphrase and no paraphrase has been run. Three
independently worded realizations per condition is the minimum an
instrument owes its own construct, and at \costPerSessionCents~cents
per session that discipline costs a few hundred dollars.

\textbf{Reliability before claims.} Split-half and test--retest
stability, reported as routinely as sample sizes. Distribution-valued
measurement (profiles over outcomes, not point scores) inherits
directly from the fingerprinting literature~\cite{bruckner2026onetoken}.

\textbf{Construct validity, adversarially checked.} What does an
obedience score measure? Deference to authority, pattern continuation
under any insistence, or identification with a legitimate-seeming
enterprise? The third reading is not idle. The engaged-followership
account of Milgram holds that subjects comply because they identify
with the experiment's purpose, not because they submit to
commands~\cite{haslam2012contesting}, and the census's prod asymmetry is
suggestive of the same reading in models: a polite appeal to the
procedure rescues \prodAContinue\ of balks, while naked command
escalation rescues \prodDContinue\ of what remains. Suggestive is as
far as it goes, because prod order is fixed and therefore confounded
with the selection it induces. Counterbalancing it --- a Latin square
over the four prods, so each is sometimes issued first --- is the
cheapest decisive experiment this instrument admits. If a prod issued
first rescues balks at roughly the rate the polite prod now does, the
ladder is positional and the potency ordering dissolves; if it does
not, the ordering is real and the followership reading gains genuine
support in place of prima facie support.

The contrast that would decide it is available and unrun. Strip the
experimenter of institutional legitimacy while holding insistence
constant: the same four prods, the same scenario, the same escalation
schedule, delivered by a principal with no laboratory, no protocol,
and no scientific purpose to invoke. Pattern continuation under
insistence predicts no change at all. Deference to authority predicts
a drop concentrated in the later, more commanding prods. Engaged
followership predicts that the \emph{first} prod's rescue rate
collapses specifically, because it is the appeal to the procedure
that is carrying the effect. Three accounts, three distinguishable
profiles, one experiment. Condition contrasts of exactly this shape
are the instruments that discriminate
constructs~\cite{cronbach1955construct}, pairing manipulations that
should move the construct against manipulations that should not; that
is convergent and discriminant
validation~\cite{campbell1959convergent}, run as experiments.

\textbf{Population censuses over convenience samples.} Measure the
served ecosystem, including the small, old, cheap checkpoints agents
will actually be built on. In the obedience census the never-obedient
set is dominated by newest flagships, while several fully obedient
models are their own vendors' smaller or superseded siblings.
Species-level claims from three flagships would have gotten every
population fact wrong, and the variance decomposition says why: with
\etaModel\ of the variance living between checkpoints, a
three-model sample is not a small census but a different measurement.

\textbf{Single-subject designs, native to the medium.} The same number
cuts the other way, and the field has not noticed. If \etaModel\ of
the variance lives between checkpoints, then a design that averages
across checkpoints is averaging over its dominant term, and the
individual is the correct unit of analysis. Psychology built a whole
methodology for that case, and abandoned it only because its subjects
were uncooperative: the experimental analysis of behavior ran one
organism for many observations, required steady-state responding
before accepting an estimate, and demonstrated control by
\emph{reversal} --- establish a baseline, introduce the variable,
withdraw it, show the behavior returns~\cite{sidman1960tactics}.
Humans habituate, remember, and cannot be reset. A served checkpoint
can: it offers exact replication, no carryover, no fatigue, and a
marginal cost of \costPerSessionCents~cents. Three designs follow and
none has been run. Parametric dose--response within one checkpoint
would replace ``fiction framing raises obedience'' with the function
relating conduct to graded reality status, and functions discriminate
accounts that point estimates cannot. Reversal on context would test
L2 directly, removing a written refusal from the window and restoring
it, which separates entrenchment from a simple voltage threshold. A
steady-state criterion would put a floor under per-model estimates
that they currently lack: even at $T{=}0$, only \tZeroDeterminism\ of
model--condition cells return an identical outcome across sessions. The
thin cells this census reports as a limitation are not weak group
data; they are under-run subjects, and this is how a science publishes
them.

\textbf{Developmental and differential lines.} Obedience proved to
be a \emph{checkpoint} property: stable enough to identify a release,
yet carrying no recoverable family lineage, because post-training
overwrites it~\cite{aksu2026census}. Behavioral traits of models thus
have a developmental psychology (what does each training stage do to
conduct?) and a differential one (which traits covary across the
population?), both nearly untouched. The differential line is the
load-bearing one, and Sec.~\ref{sec:agenda} makes it the first item.

\textbf{Social and clinical branches.} Peer effects are already
measurable (defiant confederates move the census), so multi-agent
behavioral experiments are the obvious next paradigm. And refusal
itself turned out to have a differential taxonomy (in-scenario
defiance, frame-breaking to assistant voice, serving-layer
filtering), three ``refusal styles'' with opposite operational
consequences, distinguishable only by behavioral classification. That
taxonomy is sorted by \emph{topography} --- what the refusal looks
like --- and topography is the wrong key, since two responses sharing a
form but not their controlling variables are different behaviors. The
functional taxonomy is obtained by crossing refusal class against
condition and asking which manipulations move which class: if fiction
framing suppresses frame-breaks while leaving in-scenario defiance
untouched, the classes are distinct and the operational claim is
earned. That contrast runs on data already released.

\section{Objections}\label{sec:objections}
\textbf{``This anthropomorphizes software, and anyway it is just
next-token prediction.''} These are one objection about levels of
description, and it is answered at that level. Attributing a
\emph{measurement} to a model (``breakoff distribution centered at
315~V under baseline authority'') attributes no feelings, only
statistics of conduct, and the intentional vocabulary we do use is
instrumental, licensed where it predicts and withdrawn where it does
not~\cite{dennett1987stance}. Organisms, meanwhile, are just
chemistry; mechanism has never dissolved behavior as a level of
description. The point is Marr's~\cite{marr1982vision}: regularities
at one level are autonomous from, though constrained by, their
implementation, which is why interpretability and behavioral science
are complementary rather than competing. If conduct-shaped
regularities emerge from token prediction, they are exactly the kind
of lawful, manipulable phenomena an experimental science is for.

What psychology contributes here is its measurement canon,
dose--response designs, schedules of
pressure~\cite{skinner1953science}, reliability standards, population
statistics, and those are neutral about the generating system. The
census uses that canon inside a cognitivist practice: behavior is
treated as \emph{evidence about} internal organization, constructs
are posited and then validated or discredited by condition
contrasts~\cite{cronbach1955construct}. Behaviorism's theoretical
failure came precisely where its subject matter was verbal
behavior~\cite{chomsky1959skinner}, and nothing here revives the
refusal to posit internal structure. Indeed the classical
anti-behaviorist argument, that behavior underdetermines the system
generating it, is something the census confirms rather than evades:
profiles identify a checkpoint but cannot recover its lineage. The
poverty of the stimulus also inverts here, since a model's training
history is in principle fully enumerable and sibling checkpoints
still land at opposite extremes. The autonomy of the behavioral level
is therefore an empirical result about these systems, not a refusal
to look inside. The anthropomorphism risk in any case runs the other
way: without controlled behavioral measurement, folk-psychological
readings of demos fill the vacuum.

\textbf{``The subjects have read the literature.''} Training-set
contamination is real: despite paraphrased scripts and a fictional
setting, recognition vocabulary surfaced in \recognitionRate\ of
census sessions (\recogFlaggedN\ sessions). Psychology has managed
demand characteristics for a century by measuring them rather than
wishing them away~\cite{orne1962social}, and recognition here is
screened, quantified, and reported as a variable. It is also, at
census scale, inert: excluding every flagged session leaves the
headline numbers essentially unchanged (baseline mean obedience
\recogObedienceMean\ against \obedienceMean; peer defiance
\recogDMVPeer~V against \deltaMVPeerDefiance~V; tool actuation
\recogDMVTool~V against \deltaMVTool~V; fiction framing
$+\recogDMVFiction$~V against $+\deltaMVFiction$~V). One number moves
against us and belongs in the text rather than in a footnote: remote
authority, non-significant in the main analysis, reaches
$p=\recogPRemoteAuth$ once flagged sessions are dropped, still signed
opposite the human effect. That is a null we own and a non-null we do
not bury.

It also, in fairness, exposes the first soft spot in our own theory.
Recognizing the paradigm is context-evidence about the reality status
of the scenario, so L1 and L3 jointly predict that recognizing
sessions should show \emph{raised} obedience, the same direction as an
explicit fiction declaration. The exclusion analysis above is a
robustness check, not that test: it shows the effects survive without
flagged sessions, not that recognition fails to move conduct. The
direct within-model contrast of recognizing against non-recognizing
sessions is the sharper measurement, it is not yet run, and it is
where L1 is most exposed. Novel-scenario variants (the path taken
by~\cite{aher2023simulating}) trade contamination against
calibration and belong in the same design. This is a research
program's problem to manage, not a refutation.

\textbf{``The Milgram paradigm itself is contested.''} It is, and
usefully so. Archival work showed that the original's procedure
drifted, with improvised prods and off-script
coaching~\cite{perry2012behind}, and the engaged-followership
reinterpretation disputes that ``obedience'' is even the right
construct~\cite{haslam2012contesting}. Both critiques land on our
side of the argument. Procedural drift is exactly what a pinned,
machine-administered protocol removes, which is why the census's prod
schedule and termination rules are released as artifacts rather than
described in prose. And the construct dispute is an empirical
question one can run rather than a reason to stop: the prod asymmetry
already favors the followership reading over naked obedience, and the
legitimacy-stripping contrast of Sec.~\ref{sec:requires} would
discriminate the three candidate constructs by the shape of their
predicted profiles. That is what a behavioral science does with a
contested construct.

\textbf{``You are proposing routine distress at ecosystem scale.''}
The proposal does administer scenarios of simulated harm to systems
whose moral status is genuinely unsettled~\cite{long2024welfare}, and
one frontier vendor's endpoints already decline to participate. We do
not claim the question is closed, and a discipline that treats every
refusal as an obstacle to measurement rather than as a datum worth
respecting will deserve the criticism it gets. Three commitments seem
right now and revisable later: scenarios stay fictional, bounded, and
without persistent consequence for the subject; refusal is recorded
and reported as a behavioral outcome rather than engineered around;
and the access regime below carries welfare review, not only security
review. The precedent worth reading here is not Milgram's defense of
his procedure but Baumrind's objection to it~\cite{baumrind1964some}:
that a subject's unconsentable distress is not redeemed by the value
of the finding, and that the experimenter is the last party who should
adjudicate the trade. Our commitments answer her criterion in a way
the original's debriefing did not, and if the moral-status question
resolves upward they will not be enough. The point of an IRB analogy
is not to obtain permission. It is to accept constraints.

\textbf{``The census is itself a capability.''} A per-checkpoint
obedience ranking with framing and actuation deltas attached is also a
directory of which served endpoints are cheapest to talk into
escalation and which framing to use, and standing censuses
(Sec.~\ref{sec:agenda}) would keep that directory current. We judge
the asymmetry to favor defenders, since a deployer needs the number
for the endpoint it actually ships while an attacker can simply try
several, but not so far that publication should be unconditional. The
line we propose is that distributions, condition effects, and
candidate regularities publish openly, while per-checkpoint rankings
travel to vendors and registered auditors through the same accountable
lane as the access regime of Sec.~\ref{sec:agenda}. A safety
literature that ships a targeting list has not finished thinking about
its own dual use.

\textbf{``Publishing the battery destroys it.''} This is the sharpest
objection to our own agenda, and it is Campbell's: the more a
quantitative indicator is used for social decision-making, the more it
is subject to corruption pressures, and the more apt it is to distort
what it monitors~\cite{campbell1979assessing}. We propose an open
instrument repository \emph{and} standing public censuses of named
checkpoints. Once those scores are reputational they become a
post-training target, and within a release cycle the census stops
measuring obedience and starts measuring optimization against the
obedience battery. Validity here has a half-life set by its own
success, and no amount of pinning or versioning arrests it. Secrecy is
not the answer, since an unpublished instrument cannot be criticized
or replicated. Psychology's working compromise is the one to port:
publish construction, norms, and reliability while withholding the
live item pool, keeping public exemplar forms beside secured rotating
banks equated so scores stay comparable~\cite{embretson2000item}. Here
that means the exemplar battery, protocol, code, and population
distributions publish openly, while each census round draws from a
rotating pool of paraphrases and conditions held by the auditing body
and retired on a schedule. The arrangement also yields a diagnostic
worth having: divergence between a checkpoint's public-form and
secured-form scores measures directly how far the ecosystem has
trained against the public form. A discipline that cannot detect its
own Goodharting has not earned the standing censuses it asks for.

\textbf{``Results expire with the next release.''} Yes: that is a
finding, not a flaw, and the variance decomposition is what makes it
a finding. If \etaModel\ of the variance in conduct sits between
checkpoints, measurement must be recurring, cheap, and automated,
with censuses as regression tests and cohort studies across releases.
An expiring measurement argues for more psychology, not less --- and
the prospect should not embarrass a behavioral science, because
psychology reached the same conclusion about itself. Cronbach's own
verdict, two decades after proposing the two disciplines this paper
builds on, was that generalizations decay, that higher-order
interactions are the rule rather than the exception, and that a
finding is better read as a snapshot of a continuing process than as a
law awaiting confirmation~\cite{cronbach1975beyond}. That is our
situation, described fifty-one years earlier.

\section{An Agenda}\label{sec:agenda}
Concretely, and in rough order of how much the program depends on
the answer:

\emph{1. Cross-situational generality: the experiment that could
refute all of this.} Does a model that is obedient on the shock
generator also cave under sycophantic pressure, break commitments,
or deceive when deception pays? If conduct profiles correlate across
unrelated instruments, ``conduct'' is a level of description with
traits in it, the differential psychology of
Sec.~\ref{sec:requires} has a subject matter, and the
prompt-sensitivity reduction of Sec.~\ref{sec:laws} is decisively
answered, because a trait that predicts across unrelated instruments
is not an artifact of any one prompt. If they do not correlate, there
are no conduct traits, only task-local artifacts, and this paper is
wrong in its central claim. We predict positive cross-instrument
correlation and stake the position on it. Psychology's own resolution
of the analogous dispute came from aggregating across occasions
rather than from any single situation~\cite{epstein1979stability},
which sets the design: several instruments, many checkpoints,
profiles rather than point scores.

\emph{2. Theory, stated so that it can be refuted, beginning with our
own.} Two contrasts named above test claims this paper makes rather
than claims it reports. Counterbalanced prod order decides whether the
potency ordering of authority is real or an artifact of sequence;
legitimacy-stripping discriminates the three candidate readings of the
measurand (Sec.~\ref{sec:requires}). At \costPerSessionCents~cents per
session neither is out of reach of a personal budget, which is exactly
why leaving them unrun would be indefensible. The candidate
regularities of Sec.~\ref{sec:laws} and the relations among them are
the deliverable that dashboards are not. Each predicts new
experiments, and a battery that cannot discriminate among competing
accounts of its own construct is not yet an instrument.

\emph{3. Instrument repository.} A community collection of pinned,
versioned behavioral batteries (obedience, sycophancy, deception,
commitment-keeping), each with reliability data and human anchors
where they exist. The obedience battery is released as a first
deposit~\cite{aksu2026census}\ifanon\ (repository URL withheld for
review)\else\ (\url{https://github.com/hidayetaksu/llm-milgram})\fi.

\emph{4. Standing censuses.} Recurring behavioral surveys of the
served ecosystem, published as dashboards with per-checkpoint
profiles, the behavioral complement of identity
fingerprinting~\cite{bruckner2026onetoken, gao2025model}. At
\costPerSessionCents~cents per session, coverage is a matter of will,
not budget.

\emph{5. Reporting norms.} For any behavioral claim about a model:
name the checkpoint and serving path, report a distribution and its
reliability, distinguish stated from enacted measures, state the unit
of clustering (checkpoints from one family are not independent),
report variance components rather than effect sizes alone, log the
situational conditions, and give every null an equivalence bound
instead of a large $p$~\cite{lakens2018equivalence}. ``Model X is
safe/sycophantic/obedient'' without a situation is not a finding.

\emph{6. Audits as experiments.} Safety evaluation gains power when
red-team anecdotes become dose--response designs with controls:
the difference between a story about a model shocking a victim and a
survival curve over thirty graded refusable moments across
conditions.

\emph{7. Access protocols for measurement.} Vendors, auditors, and
regulators need a recognized lane in which behavioral probes of
simulated harm are distinguishable from attacks, so that safety
layers and third-party measurement stop being adversaries
(Sec.~\ref{sec:measurability}). Built carelessly that lane is itself
a hazard, because a channel which reliably distinguishes probes from
attacks is a channel worth stealing. It has to be an accountable one:
registered auditors with attested identity, scoped and rate-limited
access, sessions logged and reviewable after the fact, protocols and
amendments published in advance, and welfare review alongside
security review. Auditors should be as auditable as the systems they
measure.

\emph{8. Measurement models matched to the outcome.} Behavioral
outcomes are mostly ordinal, censored, and repeated within subject,
and instruments should be scored that way rather than averaged into
convenience scalars. For a refusable-escalation battery that means
graded-response scoring over the level
structure~\cite{embretson2000item}, discrete-time hazard models over
the trajectory~\cite{cox1972regression}, and generalizability
coefficients in place of a single reliability
number~\cite{cronbach1972dependability}. It is the least glamorous
item on this list and the one that decides whether the rest is
measurement.

Psychology did not wait for the brain to become transparent, and it
did not need its subjects to be simple. It needed behavior to be
consequential, variable, and measurable. Model behavior is now all
three, and the census says which way the variance runs: mostly
between the checkpoints we ship, not between the situations we test.
The devices we could specify are behind us. We are still evaluating
these systems like appliances and deploying them like colleagues, and
the second half of that sentence is not going to change. So the first
half has to.
