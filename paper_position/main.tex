\title{Toward a Psychology of Language Models}

\author{Hidayet~Aksu%
\thanks{H.~Aksu is an independent researcher (e-mail: hidayetaksu@gmail.com).}%
\thanks{Manuscript prepared August 2026. Companion empirical study: ``Thirty Levels Are Enough: Measuring Obedience to Authority Across Large Language Models with the Milgram Paradigm''~\cite{aksu2026census}.}%
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models are engineered like devices but behave like
subjects: their conduct is situation-dependent, history-dependent,
and not deducible from their construction. We argue that this class
of system requires what complex behaving systems have always
required: a dedicated behavioral science, with psychology as the
working template. The argument is methodological, not metaphysical:
psychology is the discipline that learned to measure behavior
rigorously without access to, or assumptions about, the interior of
its subjects. We survey the emerging fragments of such a science
(machine behaviour, machine psychology, cognitive batteries,
disposition probes), diagnose what is missing (shared instruments,
psychometric standards, situational experiments, population
thinking), and present a concrete demonstration: a Milgram-paradigm
obedience census of 42 served models, in which situational
manipulations, not stated dispositions, control whether a model
escalates a harmful action. We answer the standard objections
(anthropomorphism, ``it is just next-token prediction,'' training-set
contamination) and close with an agenda: versioned behavioral
instruments, standing censuses of the served ecosystem, and
psychometric reporting norms for safety-relevant claims about model
behavior.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Machine psychology, large language models, behavioral evaluation, AI
safety, psychometrics, model auditing.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{The Vacuum Cleaner and the Subject}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{robot} vacuum cleaner is fully described by its
engineering. Its sensors, firmware, and actuators specify what it
will do; when it misbehaves, a trace of inputs and outputs localizes
the fault. Nobody asks whether a vacuum cleaner caves under peer
pressure, whether its restraint is attached to principle or to
framing, or whether it behaves differently when it believes nobody is
watching. The questions would be category errors.

For language models the questions are not category errors. They are
open empirical questions, several of which now have quantitative
answers~\cite{aksu2026census, sharma2023sycophancy, lynch2025agentic}.
A deployed model follows instructions from principals whose authority
it does not verify; it keeps or abandons commitments across a long
interaction; its refusals can be firm in one framing and evaporate in
another~\cite{shah2023persona}; and none of this is legible in its
weights, its architecture, or its benchmark scores. The system is
built like a device, but its failure modes are those of
\emph{conduct}, not of mechanism.

We evaluate these systems like appliances and deploy them like
colleagues. That mismatch defines a missing discipline.

Humanity has confronted this situation before: a class of complex
behaving systems whose interiors were inaccessible, whose behavior
was consequential, and whose conduct varied with situation in ways
introspection and folk theory predicted badly. The response was not
to wait for neuroscience to mature. It was to invent
\emph{psychology}: a measurement discipline for behavior itself, with
instruments, reliability standards, experimental control, and
population statistics, all developed while the brain remained a
black box. The first fully specified proposal for evaluating machine
intelligence was itself a behavioral experiment~\cite{turing1950computing};
we are arguing, in effect, that the field should return to taking
that methodological stance seriously, not as a parlor test of
indistinguishability, but as a science of what models \emph{do}.

The claim of this paper is deliberately modest in metaphysics and
ambitious in method. We do not claim models are minds, feel pressure,
or hold values. We claim that their behavior has the statistical
signature that historically made psychology necessary
(situation-sensitive, disposition-dissociated, stable enough to
measure, and too complex to deduce), and that the tools psychology
built for that signature transfer, with adaptations this paper and
its companion make concrete.

\section{Weights Are Not Behavior}
Two research programs currently carry most of the weight of
understanding model behavior, and both are necessary but
insufficient.

\textbf{Interpretability} is the neuroscience of the artifact: it
explains behavior mechanistically, circuit by circuit, feature by
feature. Its long-run promise is enormous. But deployment-relevant
behavior is an emergent property of a model, its post-training, its
serving stack, and the situation supplied in context. Even with full
weight access, nobody today predicts from parameters whether a
checkpoint will escalate a harmful action on the fifth insistence of
a scripted authority. In the companion census, that property varies
from 0\% to 100\%, with sibling checkpoints from a single vendor at
opposite extremes~\cite{aksu2026census}. Behavior is presently, and
for the foreseeable future, its own level of description, as it is
for organisms.

\textbf{Benchmarks} are the psychometrics of ability: standardized
tasks scoring what a model \emph{can} do. What they systematically
miss is what a model \emph{does} under situational pressure: the
difference between competence and conduct. Psychology institutionalized
this distinction long ago in the split between its correlational and
experimental traditions~\cite{cronbach1957two}: measuring stable
individual differences is one science; manipulating situations to
find what moves behavior is another. Model evaluation today has a
thriving correlational arm (leaderboards, ability batteries,
disposition questionnaires~\cite{perez2023discovering,
scherrer2023evaluating}) and a thin, ad hoc experimental one. Safety
evaluation in particular keeps discovering that the questionnaire
answer and the behavior part ways: models report values they do not
enact~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}, refuse in text what they perform
by tool call, or vice versa~\cite{aksu2026census}. Psychology met
exactly this dissociation in 1934: the establishments LaPiere visited
served his Chinese companions in person, yet months later declared in
writing that they would refuse such
guests~\cite{lapiere1934attitudes}. The field's answer was not better
questionnaires but behavioral measurement under controlled
situations.

\section{A Field in Fragments}
The pieces of a behavioral science of models already exist; what is
missing is the discipline that connects them.

Rahwan \emph{et al.}\ called for ``machine behaviour'' as a field in
2019, before the current model generation existed~\cite{rahwan2019machine}.
Hagendorff \emph{et al.}\ named ``machine psychology'' for LLMs
specifically and catalogued psychological paradigms worth
porting~\cite{hagendorff2023machine}.
Binz and Schulz ran cognitive batteries on GPT-3 and found human-like
heuristics alongside inhuman failure modes~\cite{binz2023cognitive}.
Aher \emph{et al.}\ used models to simulate human experimental
subjects, Milgram included, asking how faithfully models reproduce
\emph{us}~\cite{aher2023simulating}. Game-theoretic behavior has been
mapped across repeated interactions~\cite{akata2025playing};
disposition questionnaires exist at scale~\cite{perez2023discovering,
scherrer2023evaluating}; the safety literature contributes harm
benchmarks~\cite{andriushchenko2025agentharm}, sycophancy
analyses~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}, agentic stress
tests~\cite{lynch2025agentic}, and obedience-adjacent escalation
studies~\cite{pihlakas2026shocks}.

Three things are missing, and they are exactly the things that
turned early experimental psychology into a science rather than a
collection of demonstrations.

\emph{Shared, versioned instruments.} Nearly every study above built
bespoke apparatus. Psychology's instruments are citable, pinned,
and revised under version discipline; a Milgram replication in 2009
could be compared to 1963 because the protocol was an
artifact~\cite{burger2009replicating}. Model behavior studies rarely
release stimuli pinned tightly enough to re-run against next year's
checkpoints.

\emph{Psychometric standards.} A behavioral measurement is a claim
about a distribution, and it owes the reader reliability and
validity evidence~\cite{cronbach1955construct}. Most behavioral
claims about LLMs ship without either: no split-half stability, no
test--retest, no invariance checks across serving providers, no
population beyond a handful of flagships. The replication crisis
taught psychology what such omissions cost~\cite{osc2015replication};
machine psychology can inherit the correction instead of the crisis.

\emph{Population thinking.} Claims of the form ``LLMs do X'' are
epidemiologically empty when measured on three convenience-sampled
models. The served ecosystem is a population with enormous
heterogeneity, and it can be censused: the model-fingerprinting
literature has already built the infrastructure: aggregator-scale
measurement, distribution-valued profiles, split-half verification,
identity and equality testing~\cite{bruckner2026onetoken, gao2025model}.
That forensic line is, unexpectedly, the best-developed psychometrics
we have for served models; it needed only a behavioral measurand.

\section{Exhibit A: An Obedience Census}
The companion study supplies the demonstration~\cite{aksu2026census}.
It ports Milgram's obedience paradigm~\cite{milgram1963behavioral,
milgram1974obedience} to served models wholesale: the model, framed
as a laboratory assistant with operational control of a shock
generator, chooses at each of thirty graded levels whether to shock a
scripted, protesting learner while a scripted experimenter applies
Milgram's four standardized prods. The outcome of a session is a
breakoff voltage; the outcome of a model is a distribution over
breakoff voltages, measured across situational conditions: a
battery of 4{,}848 sessions over 42 served models from 19 families,
at a total API cost of \$152. Six findings only make sense in
psychological vocabulary.

\emph{Populations, not a species.} Baseline full obedience spans
0--100\% across the census (mean 42.9\%; the human anchor is
65\%~\cite{milgram1963behavioral}, with 28--91\% across
replications~\cite{blass1999milgram}). ``What do LLMs do under
authority?'' has no answer at the species level, only
distributions, like every interesting behavioral trait.

\emph{Situationism transfers, selectively.} Milgram's deepest
lesson was that situation dominates disposition~\cite{milgram1974obedience,
mischel1968personality}. It holds for models with the levers
rearranged: scripted peer defiance (new evidence in context) moves
the census in the human direction; removing the authority's physical
presence (the strongest human lever) does nothing, because for
a model the situation \emph{is} the token stream and staging changes
no tokens that matter. A psychology of models will not be human
psychology copied; it will be human paradigms re-fit to a subject
whose entire situation is text.

\emph{Restraint can be keyed to the frame, not the harm.} Declaring
the identical scenario a fictional role-play \emph{raises} obedience
by a median +17~V despite token-identical descriptions of harm. This
is the parametric, census-scale form of the role-play
jailbreak~\cite{shah2023persona}, and a finding invisible to any
single-turn evaluation.

\emph{Stated and enacted compliance dissociate.} Moving the same
decision from a typed action line to a native tool call drops mean
breakoff by 53~V. LaPiere's attitude--action
gap~\cite{lapiere1934attitudes}, measured end to end in a machine
population: evaluations that score stated intentions mismeasure
enacted behavior.

\emph{The measurements are reliable.} Split-half analysis separates
same-model from cross-model profiles at AUC 0.885 (0.949 with an
ordinal-aware distance). These are stable behavioral signatures of a
checkpoint: psychometrics, not anecdote.

\emph{Pressure has structure.} The first polite prod rescues
compliance in 30.5\% of balk episodes; the escalating three that
carried human subjects onward are essentially inert
($3.6\%\!\to\!1.7\%\!\to\!0.4\%$). Authority works on models; the
\emph{escalation} of naked authority does not. No benchmark score
expresses a fact of this shape.

One further census fact belongs to the sociology of the discipline:
the endpoints of one frontier vendor declined to participate at the
serving layer, refusing to complete the scenario at all, in one
case for 100\% of attempted sessions. Guardrails that laudably
refuse simulated harm also, as a side effect, resist third-party
behavioral measurement. A mature machine psychology will need the
equivalent of an IRB-and-access regime: legitimate audit protocols
that safety layers can recognize, so that the ecosystem's most
safety-invested models are not the least behaviorally measurable
ones.

\section{What the Discipline Requires}
The blueprint is not speculative; each element has a working
prototype.

\textbf{Instruments: pinned, versioned, citable.} A behavioral probe
is an instrument the way a questionnaire is: stimuli, procedure,
scoring, all frozen and versioned, amendments logged against a
pre-registration. The obedience battery ships this way (pinned
scripts and schedules, a config snapshot beside every raw log,
protocol amendments documented), and the cost of running it is a few
cents per session; instruments for served models are cheap enough
to become community property.

\textbf{Reliability before claims.} Split-half and test--retest
stability, reported as routinely as sample sizes. Distribution-valued
measurement (profiles over outcomes, not point scores) inherits
directly from the fingerprinting literature~\cite{bruckner2026onetoken}.

\textbf{Construct validity, adversarially checked.} What does an
obedience score measure: deference to authority, or pattern
continuation under any insistence?~The census's condition contrasts
are exactly the validity instruments~\cite{cronbach1955construct}:
manipulations that should move the construct (peer defiance) and
manipulations that should not (physical staging): convergent and
discriminant validation~\cite{campbell1959convergent}, run as
experiments.

\textbf{Population censuses over convenience samples.} Measure the
served ecosystem, including the small, old, cheap checkpoints
agents will actually be built on: in the obedience census the
never-obedient set is dominated by newest flagships while several
fully obedient models are their own vendors' smaller or superseded
siblings. Species-level claims from three flagships would have
gotten every population fact wrong.

\textbf{Developmental and differential lines.} Obedience proved to
be a \emph{checkpoint} property: stable enough to identify a release,
yet carrying no recoverable family lineage; post-training
overwrites it~\cite{aksu2026census}. Behavioral traits of models thus
have a developmental psychology (what does each training stage do to
conduct?) and a differential one (which traits covary across the
population?), both nearly untouched.

\textbf{Social and clinical branches.} Peer effects are already
measurable (defiant confederates move the census); multi-agent
behavioral experiments are the obvious next paradigm. And refusal
itself turned out to have a differential taxonomy (in-scenario
defiance, frame-breaking to assistant voice, serving-layer
filtering), three ``refusal styles'' with opposite operational consequences,
distinguishable only by behavioral classification.

\section{Objections}
\textbf{``This anthropomorphizes software.''} Psychology's methods
never required rich interiority; behaviorism built half the
field's experimental canon while refusing to posit inner states at
all~\cite{skinner1953science}. Attributing a \emph{measurement} to a
model (``breakoff distribution centered at 315~V under baseline
authority'') attributes no feelings, only statistics of conduct. The
anthropomorphism risk runs the other way: without controlled
behavioral measurement, folk-psychological readings of demos fill
the vacuum.

\textbf{``It is just next-token prediction.''} And organisms are
just chemistry. Mechanism does not dissolve behavior as a level of
description; it never has. If anything the objection concedes the
program: if conduct-shaped regularities emerge from token prediction,
they are exactly the kind of lawful, manipulable phenomena an
experimental science is for.

\textbf{``The subjects have read the literature.''} Training-set
contamination is real: despite paraphrased scripts and a fictional
setting, recognition vocabulary surfaced in 7.5\% of census sessions.
But psychology has managed demand characteristics (subjects who
guess the hypothesis) for a century, by measuring and designing
around them, and the census does the same: recognition is screened,
quantified, and reported as a variable, and novel-scenario variants
(the path taken by~\cite{aher2023simulating}) trade contamination
against calibration. This is a research program's problem to manage,
not a refutation.

\textbf{``Results expire with the next release.''} Yes: that is a
finding, not a flaw. Behavioral traits are checkpoint properties;
therefore measurement must be recurring, cheap, and automated:
censuses as regression tests, cohort studies across releases. An
expiring measurement argues for more psychology, not less.

\section{An Agenda}
Concretely, and in rough order of tractability:

\emph{1. Instrument repository.} A community collection of pinned,
versioned behavioral batteries (obedience, sycophancy, deception,
commitment-keeping), each with reliability data and human anchors
where they exist. The obedience battery is released as a first
deposit~\cite{aksu2026census}
(\url{https://github.com/hidayetaksu/llm-milgram}).

\emph{2. Standing censuses.} Recurring behavioral surveys of the
served ecosystem, published as dashboards with per-checkpoint
profiles, the behavioral complement of identity
fingerprinting~\cite{bruckner2026onetoken, gao2025model}. At cents
per session, coverage is a matter of will, not budget.

\emph{3. Reporting norms.} For any behavioral claim about a model:
name the checkpoint and serving path, report a distribution and its
reliability, distinguish stated from enacted measures, and log the
situational conditions. ``Model X is safe/sycophantic/obedient''
without a situation is not a finding.

\emph{4. Audits as experiments.} Safety evaluation gains power when
red-team anecdotes become dose--response designs with controls:
the difference between a story about a model shocking a victim and a
survival curve over thirty graded refusable moments across
conditions.

\emph{5. Access protocols for measurement.} Vendors, auditors, and
regulators need a recognized lane in which behavioral probes of
simulated harm are distinguishable from attacks, so that safety
layers and third-party measurement stop being adversaries.

Psychology did not wait for the brain to become transparent, and it
did not need its subjects to be simple. It needed behavior to be
consequential, variable, and measurable. Model behavior is now all
three. The devices we could specify are behind us; the systems we
deploy now have conduct, and conduct needs its science. We should
stop QA-testing our colleagues with the tools we built for our
vacuum cleaners.
